Executive Summary 
This technical report presents a scientifically structured framework for developing a district-scale Digital Soil 
Mapping (DSM) and Gat -wise Digital Soil Health Card system for Pune District using an integrated Google 
Earth Engine (GEE) and Python workflow . The framework combines quality -controlled laboratory soil 
observations, systematically designed additional field sampling, satellite observations, terrain, climate, 
land-cover and parent -material information, followed by spatially robust machine -learning modelling and 
uncertainty assessment. The methodology is explicitly grounded in the SCORPAN framework and 
incorporates lessons from the systematic review of Indian DSM by Dash et al. (2022).  
A central component is representative and adaptive sampling rather than a fixed samples -per-hectare rule. 
Existing observations will first be audited for coordinate quality, depth consistency, analytical methods, units, 
duplication, outliers and spatial cl ustering. Environmental representativeness will then be assessed, and 
cLHS or partitioned/stratified cLHS will be used where justified. Bhattacharyya distance and/or Jensen –
Shannon divergence will quantify feature-space representation, while learning curves based on R², RMSE and 
MAE will support the practical sample -size decision. This directly addresses the Indian DSM gaps identified 
by Dash et al. (2022), including poor sampling density, limited independent validation, limited comparative 
modelling and insufficient uncertainty mapping. 
GEE will be used for satellite, terrain and climate covariate preparation and extraction at soil locations. 
Python will be used for cLHS, feature -space analysis, sample -size optimization, machine -learning 
comparison, spatial validation and uncertainty anal ysis. Final validated predictions will be generated at the 
selected analysis resolution and intersected with authoritative Gat/parcel boundaries to provide transparent 
Gat-wise soil information, clearly distinguishing laboratory observations from model estimates. 
Evidence Base and Methodological Rationale 
The present revision incorporates the systematic review by Dash, Panigrahi and Mishra (2022), “Identifying 
opportunities to improve digital soil mapping in India”, published in Geoderma Regional. The review screened 
35 peer -reviewed SCORPAN -based DSM studi es in India and identified recurring gaps that are directly 
relevant to the proposed Pune District implementation.

1. Project Vision 
To establish a scientifically validated Pune District Digital Soil Intelligence Platform that converts laboratory 
soil observations, satellite data and environmental information into spatially explicit, Gat -wise digital soil 
information accessible to farmers and agricultural institutions. 
2. Main Goal 
To develop Digital Soil Maps for priority soil -health parameters across Pune District and create a digital 
service in which an authorized user can retrieve Gat -wise soil information by selecting a village and Gat 
number.  
3. Specific Objectives 
1. Ingest, clean, and harmonize all existing village -level soil sample datasets into a georeferenced Master 
Soil Database. 
2. Develop a cloud-based environmental covariate extraction pipeline in GEE covering all SCORPAN factors 
for Pune District. 
3. Use cLHS or partitioned/stratified cLHS where justified by environmental heterogeneity. 
4. Determine the mathematical optimum sample size using Bhattacharyya Distance (Khan et al., 2023) and 
Jensen-Shannon Divergence (Saurette et al., 2024). 
5. Begin operational implementation in Baramati Tehsil and validate the complete end -to-end workflow 
before district-wide expansion. 
6. Train and benchmark multiple ML models (RF, SVR, XGBoost) and quantify spatial uncertainty using 
Quantile Regression Forests. 
7. Spatially intersect DSM raster outputs with cadastral Gat shapefiles in PostGIS to generate parcel -level 
statistics and automated Soil Health Cards. 
4. Detailed Sampling Methodology: cLHS and PcLHS 
Conditioned Latin Hypercube Sampling (cLHS) is an advanced stratified random sampling strategy that draws 
sample locations from multivariate environmental covariates such that the marginal distribution of each 
covariate and the multivariate correlation structure are maximally preserved (Minasny and McBratney, 2006; 
Moharana et al., 2024).

5. Scientific Framework: SCORPAN 
Digital Soil Mapping predicts the spatial distribution of soil properties by establishing relationships between 
measured soil observations and environmental variables. The conceptual basis is commonly described 
through the SCORPAN framework: soil information (S), climate (C), organisms/vegetation (O), relief (R), 
parent material (P), age/time (A) and spatial position (N) (McBratney et al., 2003).

9. Existing Soil Data Quality Control 
• Assign a permanent unique Sample_ID. 
• Validate coordinates and record coordinate reference system and positional quality where available. 
• Standardize or harmonize sampling depths while retaining original depth information. 
• Standardize units and document laboratory analytical methods. 
• Record sample date, source, village, taluka, Gat number and land-use/crop information where available. 
• Review duplicates, outliers and implausible observations using documented rules. 
• Assess spatial clustering and identify areas with insufficient representation. 
• Maintain QA/QC and chain-of-custody records for all new field observations. 
10. Sampling Design and Sample-Size Decision 
10.1 Representativeness Assessment 
Existing observations will first be projected into the selected environmental feature space. Their marginal and 
multivariate distributions will be compared with the candidate population to identify environmental and 
geographic gaps. This step determines wh ether additional sampling is necessary and where it is most 
valuable. 
10.2 Heterogeneity and Partitioning 
Pune District contains substantial environmental variation. Partitioned cLHS/PcLHS  will therefore be used 
only if analysis demonstrates that separate environmental populations or zones are justified. Regionalization 
may use physiography, agro-environmental zones, terrain/landscape clustering or other documented criteria. 
10.3 Sample-Size Optimization 
Candidate additional sample sizes will be evaluated using repeated sampling designs. Bhattacharyya 
distance and/or Jensen –Shannon divergence will assess representation, while model learning curves using 
R², RMSE and MAE will assess whether additional obser vations materially improve prediction. The selected 
sample size will balance representation, predictive stability, uncertainty, budget and field feasibility. 
No universal number such as one sample per hectare will be adopted as the scientific basis for Pune District. 
The Baramati pilot will generate an evidence-based sampling requirement for Baramati; the requirement will 
then be reviewed for each expansion unit. 

=== PAGE 8 ===
10.4 cLHS/PcLHS Point Selection 
After the sampling requirement is approved, cLHS/PcLHS  will select locations representing the chosen 
environmental covariates. Optimization settings and random seeds will be documented for reproducibility. 
Accessibility constraints will be applied after environmental selection, and inaccessible locations will  be 
replaced using nearby environmental feature-space equivalents rather than arbitrary relocation. 
11. Field and Laboratory Protocol 
• Use the project-approved standardized sampling SOP and target depth interval. 
• Record actual GPS coordinates at the sampling location. 
• Record village, taluka, Gat number, land use/crop, irrigation status where relevant, date, depth and field 
observations. 
• Use permanent Sample_ID and complete chain-of-custody documentation. 
• If a selected point is inaccessible, document the replacement and its environmental equivalence. 
• Analyze core soil-health parameters using one approved project SOP or formally harmonized laboratory 
methods. 
• Record analytical method, units, laboratory, analyst/date, detection/reporting limits where applicable 
and QA/QC information. 
• Perform duplicate and outlier review before observations enter the DSM training database.

Each soil property will normally be modelled separately. Hyperparameters will be tuned using training data

algorithm. 
• Spatial block cross-validation to reduce spatial autocorrelation bias. 
• Spatially separated independent hold-out data where feasible. 
• Metrics: R², RMSE, MAE and Bias/Mean Error. 
• Residual diagnostics and examination of model failure areas. 
• Prediction uncertainty/confidence estimation where implemented. 
• Extrapolation/domain checks to identify areas outside the training feature space. 
14. SoilGrids and Other External Soil Data 
SoilGrids and similar products are modelled estimates and will not be treated as ground truth. Pune 
laboratory observations remain the primary ground evidence, followed by validated new field observations. 
External products may be used for benchmarking, pattern com parison or as ancillary predictors only after 
independent testing and appropriate harmonization. 
15. Digital Soil Map Production 
• Select the best validated model separately for each soil property. 
• Prepare the final predictor stack for the relevant implementation unit. 
• Generate predictions over the approved analysis grid. 
• Generate uncertainty/confidence layers. 
• Perform extrapolation checks and flag low-support areas. 
• Document depth, units, time period, resolution, predictor version and model version. 
• Retain a clear distinction between observed soil values and model predictions. 
16. Gat-wise Digital Soil Health Card 
Authoritative Gat polygons will be overlaid with validated DSM layers. Because a Gat can contain multiple 
prediction cells, parcel -level statistics such as median/mean values and uncertainty measures will be 
calculated. Gat -level reporting is a reporting a nd decision -support layer; it does not imply that the soil 
property was directly measured at every location within the Gat.

20. Expansion Strategy After Baramati 
• Use Baramati to validate the complete workflow, software pipeline, database structure, field SOP and 
reporting format. 
• For every new taluka, repeat only the necessary data-audit and representativeness checks. 
• Reuse common Pune -wide covariate definitions, metadata standards, code modules and validation 
protocols. 
• Adapt sampling intensity when the new taluka has different environmental heterogeneity or legacy -data 
coverage. 
• Maintain a common model/data versioning system so taluka outputs remain traceable. 
• Integrate taluka-level outputs into the district platform only after quality and validation checks.
